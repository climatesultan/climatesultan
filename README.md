🌍 About Me

I am Gokul Rajendran, also known as Climate Sultan.

I am an Ayurveda student and self-directed learner exploring the connections between Ayurveda, biodiversity, traditional and Indigenous knowledge, climate action, culture, technology and communities.

I learn by studying deeply — and then trying to turn what I learn into something useful for people.

Rather than keeping my learning only in books and classrooms, I enjoy implementing ideas as real-world projects, digital platforms, applications, websites, media and communities.

My work sits at the intersection of:

🌿 Ayurveda & Traditional Knowledge
🌳 Biodiversity & Nature
🌍 Climate & Sustainability
🧠 Education & Knowledge Preservation
💻 Technology & Digital Platforms
🎬 Culture, Art & Storytelling
🤝 Youth & Community

🧭 My Approach

Study → Understand → Build → Share → Create Impact

I believe traditional knowledge should not remain locked inside old books or separated from modern technology.

It can be documented, digitised, taught, connected, experienced and shared responsibly with the world.

My projects are experiments in doing exactly that.

🌱 Govardhan App — Indian Plants

An Ayurveda-focused learning application built around medicinal plants and the needs of Ayurveda students.

The app contains information on 253 medicinal plants, including Sanskrit and common names, scientific names, botanical families, morphology, Ayurvedic properties, formulations and an interactive quiz system.

The app brings classical Ayurvedic learning into a modern digital format, with Sanskrit text, English meanings, audio and search functionality.

🌏 A Bigger Mission

I am interested in a future where:

traditional knowledge + modern technology + biodiversity + climate action + culture

can work together rather than existing in separate worlds.

The goal is not simply to digitise the past.

It is to explore how knowledge from different traditions can be preserved responsibly, made accessible, connected with communities and used to inspire new solutions for the future.
